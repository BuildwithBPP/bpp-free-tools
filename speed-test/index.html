    How the Speed-to-Lead Test works

    The Speed-to-Lead Test grades how your business handles a new inquiry, across every way a customer reaches you. It asks 7 scored questions about what actually happens, 2 questions about your volume and job value, and 1 question about timing. Each answer is one tap. You see your Response Grade and an estimate of what slips through each month right after the last question. You don't need to give your email for that.

    What it checks, and how much each part counts

    • Reply speed (25 points). How long the last new inquiry waited for a real reply.
    • After-hours and busy calls (20 points). What happens when nobody can pick up.
    • Website form (15 points). What someone gets right after they submit at night.
    • Quote follow-up (15 points). What happens when a quote goes quiet.
    • Texting (10 points). Whether a text to your business number gets answered.
    • Online booking (10 points). Whether a customer can take the next step without calling.
    • Coverage (5 points). Who answers when you're busy or out.

    Each answer earns from 0% to 100% of its part's points. The Response Score is the total, out of 100. Grades: A is 85 or higher, B is 70 to 84, C is 55 to 69, D is 40 to 54, and F is under 40.

    How the "what slips" estimate works

    Slip rate = a base set by your reply speed (0% when you reply in under 5 minutes, 10% within an hour, rising to about 40% at two days or more) + points for each open gap: calls that just ring (+8), or voicemail returned "when we can" (+4) or the same day (+2); web forms with no reply (+4) or a generic one (+2); quotes nobody follows up on (+5) or only when you remember (+3); texts that get missed (+3), no texting or not sure (+2); inquiries that wait for you (+2) or mostly go to you (+1); and no online booking (+1). Capped at 50%.
    Inquiries that slip = your monthly inquiries × slip rate
    Revenue at risk = inquiries that slip × first-year customer value × 25% (we assume you'd win 1 in 4 of them)

    These are working estimates, not a study. They're built to move in the right direction as you close gaps. For your own number, run the live test and retake it, or put your real close rate into the Cost-of-Owner-Dependence Calculator.

    Where the benchmarks come from

    • Replying within an hour made firms about 7 times as likely to qualify a lead; the average first reply across 2,241 companies took 42 hours (Harvard Business Review lead-response audit, reported by InsideSales). The same source puts the first responder's share of sales at 35% to 50%.
    • Only 52% of calls to home-service businesses were answered by a person (Invoca home-services benchmarks, 2026, drawn from 70 million+ calls across 10 industries).
    • In a test of 450+ home-service companies contacted through their website forms, as many as 40% didn't respond within five days (Valve+Meter, reported by Contractor).
