    How the AI Employee Readiness Assessment works

    This assessment answers two questions. Which AI assistant should your business start with? And is your business ready for it? It asks 8 questions about where your time goes and what systems you already have. It takes about 2 minutes.

    An “AI employee” here means an AI assistant set up to own one kind of repeat work. The assessment picks from five:

    • The Follow-Up Assistant works your leads and open quotes every day.
    • The Admin Assistant handles scheduling, reminders, routine emails and chasing paperwork.
    • The Proposal Writer turns your notes into a branded, priced proposal draft.
    • The Content Assistant keeps you visible online, in your voice.
    • The Numbers Copilot keeps the books current and explains them in plain English.

    The 8 questions

    1. Where does most of your week actually disappear? Pick one: scheduling, emails and repetitive admin; chasing leads and following up on quotes; writing quotes, proposals and estimates; marketing; or bookkeeping, invoices and money questions.
    2. What happens to a new lead who contacts you today? From “Honestly, leads sit for days” to “Instant response, tracked to close.”
    3. How do quotes and proposals get made? From “From scratch every time, hours each” to “We rarely need written proposals.”
    4. How visible is your business online in a typical month? From “Almost never, no time to create” to “We post consistently and it brings leads.”
    5. When did you last see a clear monthly picture of your numbers? From “I check the bank balance, that's it” to “Every month, current and clear.”
    6. What runs the business today, software-wise? From “Paper, texts, and memory” to “CRM + accounting software, used daily.”
    7. How much have you personally used AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.)? From “Skeptical, need to see proof” to “Weekly, already part of how I work.”
    8. If an assistant handed you back 5 hours a week, where would you want them taken from? Pick one of the same five areas as question 1.

    How your recommendation is calculated

    Each answer adds points to one or more of the five assistants:

    • Question 1 adds 3 points to the assistant that matches your biggest time sink.
    • Questions 2 to 5 each look at one area: leads, proposals, being visible online, and your numbers. The worse the problem, the more points that assistant gets, up to 4. If you say an area is already handled or not needed, that assistant is ruled out.
    • Question 6 adds 1 point to the Admin Assistant if the business runs on paper, texts and memory.
    • Question 8 adds 2 points to the assistant you said you want.

    The assistant with the most points is your first hire. If two are tied, the one you picked in question 8 wins. Next comes the one from question 1. After that, a fixed order: Follow-Up, Admin, Proposal, Content, Numbers. The Admin Assistant is never ruled out, so you always get a result. Your second hire is the next assistant in line, but only if your answers gave it points.

    Readiness comes from questions 6 and 7 only.

    Software (0 to 3): paper and memory 0, spreadsheets 1, accounting software plus spreadsheets 2, CRM plus accounting software used daily 3
    AI comfort (1 to 3): skeptical or curious 1, tried a few times 2, weekly use 3
    Readiness = software + AI comfort, out of 6

    How to read your result

    Your result names your first AI hire and shows one of three readiness levels:

    • Ready Now (5 or 6 points). Your systems and comfort level mean the assistant could be working inside your business within weeks.
    • Ready With Light Setup (3 or 4 points). Some groundwork comes first. If your software is weak, that means one place for clean customer and job details. If your software is strong, the setup is about trust: the assistant starts on one task while you check its work.
    • Foundations First (1 or 2 points). AI would sit on shaky ground today. One system of record and clean basics come first, then the assistant.

    You also see four jobs the assistant handles in week one, and your second hire, if there is one. Start with one assistant, and get it earning its keep before you add another.     Common questions

    What if my answers say everything is already handled?

    Then your first hire is the Admin Assistant. Every business has everyday admin around its other work, so that's the natural place to start.

    Why does it pick only one assistant to start?

    Starting with one keeps it simple. Once the first assistant is earning its keep, you'll know what to add next. Your result still names a second hire when your answers point to one.

    Do I need a CRM first?

    Not always. The readiness level tells you. A business that runs on paper and memory starts with one system of record. A business with a CRM and accounting software used daily needs light setup at most.
